That page should also establish a few concepts that will help us constantly.

### 1. Funnel stage ≠ channel

Cold calling, Lone Star Supply, search, referral, and paid ads are **channels**.

They can all feed the same funnel.

We don't want five completely different sales systems just because prospects entered from different places.

### 2. Conversion ≠ purchase

There are several conversions:

```text
stranger → engaged visitor
visitor → lead
lead → preview request
preview request → sales conversation
sales conversation → paid customer
paid customer → activated customer
activated customer → retained customer
retained customer → advocate
```

That alone is worth documenting, because it stops us from treating "conversion rate" as one number.

### 3. The hero owns the interpretive lens

This should absolutely become a rule on the page after what we were just discussing.

For each acquisition surface, define:

```text
ARRIVES
What situation are they in?

BELIEVES
What do they currently think?

LENS
How should they interpret what follows?

PROOF
What must they see to believe us?

NEXT YES
What is the lowest-friction meaningful commitment?
```

That framework could eventually become part of **how Rule & Form generates websites for customers**, not just how we market Rule & Form.

### 4. Activation needs an explicit definition

This is especially important for a subscription company.

A contractor paying \$299 is not necessarily activated.

For V1, I might define Rule & Form activation as something close to:

> **A Site is live and the Business has experienced at least one core operating loop through Rule & Form.**

For example:

```text
Site live
  +
lead received
  +
follow-up / communication visible
```

We can refine that, but having an activation definition will later matter enormously for onboarding, instrumentation, churn analysis, and product priorities.

### 5. Funnel economics eventually hang off this map

Later, every transition can get metrics:

```text
prospects reached
→ conversations
→ previews
→ closes
→ launches
→ activations
→ 30-day retained
→ 90-day retained
→ referrals
```

Then channel economics become intelligible rather than "cold calling seems to work."

---

I actually think we should eventually have **three tightly related pages**, but only write the first now:

**Acquisition & Conversion Funnel** — how someone becomes a customer. **Activation & Customer Lifecycle** — how a paid customer reaches value, stays, expands, and leaves. **Channels & Experiments** — outbound, supply houses, referrals, search, paid, codes, attribution, experiments.

That gives the commercial side the same discipline we're giving the architecture without turning Mintlify into an MBA textbook.

And the funnel page could be quite short, maybe **1,500–2,000 words max**. Its purpose is to establish the map and vocabulary so every homepage, campaign, Site Type, sales script, and onboarding decision has somewhere to attach.

**Distilled insight:** we shouldn't document "marketing." We should document the **state transitions between stranger and successful customer**. That is a system, just like provisioning or publishing.
