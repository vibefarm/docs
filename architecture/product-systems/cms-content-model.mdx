---
title: "CMS Content Model"
description: "How Rule & Form represents reusable website content as typed, provider-independent records between approved Content State, the Website Engine, and provider-specific publishing."
---

The CMS Content Model defines the structured content Rule & Form can place into websites.

It is not the Business Record.

It is not Content State.

It is not the Framer CMS schema.

It is the provider-independent content contract between Rule & Form's approved website expression and the systems that publish it.

Content State Version ↓ approved expression

↓

CMS Content Model ↓ typed reusable content records

Website Engine ↓ pages, sections, bindings, ordering

↓

Publishing Mapping ↓

Framer CMS / localization

The Website Engine answers:

What should this Site contain, and where should it appear?

The CMS Content Model answers:

What structured content does that website have available to place there?

Those are different responsibilities.

This is not another source of truth

The CMS Content Model is derived from authoritative Rule & Form state.

Business Record \+ Content State ↓ content projection ↓ CMS Content Model

Do not create a second editable version of the same business or content facts merely because publishing needs a convenient structure.

For example:

Business Record

service\_id: svc.plumbing.drain\_cleaning

offered: true

and:

Content State

service headline: Drain Cleaning Without the Runaround

service summary: approved customer-facing expression

may resolve into:

Service Content

service: svc.plumbing.drain\_cleaning

headline: Drain Cleaning Without the Runaround

summary: approved expression

The Service Content record is not independently authoritative.

If the service fact changes, change the Business Record.

If the approved wording changes, change Content State.

Then resolve the content model again.

Do not fix canonical state by hand-editing its publishing projection.

This is not a customer-facing CMS

Rule & Form is a managed product.

The CMS Content Model does not imply that contractors receive a generic CMS where they manipulate database-like content records.

Customer-facing changes should occur through intentional Rule & Form surfaces such as:

Business information Services Service areas Photos Offers Website change requests approved editable content

Those surfaces update the domain object that actually owns the information.

Business fact edit ↓ Business Record

approved wording edit ↓ Content State

website composition change ↓ Site / Website configuration

The CMS Content Model exists because publishing needs structure.

It is not the product's editing model.

Provider-independent identity

Every reusable content record has Rule & Form identity.

Conceptually:

Content Entity

id type site\_id where scoped source identity where applicable schema version fields localized fields asset references

The exact persistence representation may differ.

The invariant is that Rule & Form content can be identified without knowing anything about Framer.

Provider IDs stay outside

Avoid:

service\_content

framer\_collection\_id framer\_item\_id framer\_field\_id

as content identity.

Prefer:

content\_id: cnt\_service\_01

service\_id: svc.plumbing.drain\_cleaning

Then:

Rule & Form content identity ↓ Publishing Mapping / Provider Mapping ↓ Framer item identity

Framer IDs belong to provider state.

They do not define what the content means.

Content type is explicit

Content records use known typed shapes.

V1 content families may include:

Content type	Purpose Business Profile Content	Reusable approved business-facing identity and summaries Service Content	Structured expression for a canonical or custom offered service Location Content	Structured expression associated with an eligible service geography FAQ Content	Reusable question and approved answer Offer Content	Approved promotional content and its factual terms Project Content	Approved project or portfolio material where the Site requires it Proof Content	Credentials, warranties, business attributes, or other approved proof presentation where appropriate

V1 implements only the types required by the first Plumbing Site.

The model should not create dozens of speculative content types before a real website requires them.

Type is not Framer collection

Conceptually:

Rule & Form

Service Content

may currently publish to:

Framer

Services collection

That does not mean those concepts are identical.

A future template could publish Service Content differently.

A future provider could use an entirely different CMS model.

The Rule & Form content contract remains stable.

Service Content

Services are a central V1 content type.

A Service Content record binds approved expression to stable service identity.

Conceptually:

Service Content

content\_id service\_id or custom service identity

display name headline short summary long-form content where required supporting proof references FAQ references asset references

localized expression

The Business Record still owns whether the Business offers the service.

Business Record

offers: svc.plumbing.drain\_cleaning

The CMS Content Model does not establish that fact.

Known service identity remains canonical service\_id: svc.plumbing.water\_heater\_repair

is preferable to:

service: Water Heater Repair

as the relationship.

Visible wording can change.

The service identity remains stable.

Custom services remain possible

A legitimate custom service that does not yet exist in the Vertical Blueprint must still be representable.

The content record may reference the Business Record's custom service identity rather than inventing an incorrect canonical service.

custom business service ↓ approved Content State ↓ Service Content

A recurring custom service can later become a Vertical Blueprint candidate without rewriting historical content identity silently.

Business Profile Content

Some approved content describes the Business rather than one service.

Examples include:

short company summary about summary business story approved differentiators approved proof summary service-area summary general CTA support copy

Do not confuse this with the Business Record.

Business Record

founded\_year = 2012 family\_owned = true

may support:

Business Profile Content

"Family-owned plumbing serving Abilene since 2012."

The first is fact.

The second is expression.

That distinction remains intact even when both eventually appear in the same website section.

FAQ Content

FAQs are structured content, not arbitrary blobs appended to pages.

Conceptually:

FAQ Content

content\_id

question answer

topic service references location references where relevant eligibility metadata

localized expression

A FAQ may be reusable across more than one page.

FAQ How quickly can you respond?

↓ Homepage Drain Cleaning page Emergency service page

The Website Engine decides where the FAQ is eligible to appear.

The CMS Content Model owns the structured FAQ itself.

FAQ presence does not mean schema eligibility

The existence of FAQ content does not automatically mean a page should emit FAQ structured data.

That decision belongs to Localization & Search Structure and the Website Engine's resolved search intent.

Content existence and search markup eligibility remain separate.

Location Content

Service-area facts originate in the Business Record.

Business Record

Abilene Potosi Buffalo Gap Tuscola

That does not automatically create four Location Content pages.

service area fact ↓ search / product eligibility rules ↓ Location Content where justified

Where a location deserves website-specific expression, the CMS Content Model can represent it structurally.

Conceptually:

Location Content

content\_id geography reference

display name approved summary local context service relationships FAQ relationships asset references where relevant

localized expression

The Website Engine and search specification determine whether that content receives a page.

A structured geography does not automatically become a generated SEO page.

Content records and pages are different things

A content record is reusable content.

A Page is website structure.

Do not make one database object do both jobs.

Service Content Drain Cleaning

may appear in:

Homepage Services index Drain Cleaning page Footer service list related-services component

Likewise:

Business Profile Content

may appear in:

Homepage About page Footer Contact page

The content remains one semantic source.

The Website Engine determines each placement.

Content bindings

The Website Engine references content semantically.

Conceptually:

Homepage

Featured Services ↓ Service Content: Sewer Repair Repiping Water Heater Replacement

or:

Service Page

Primary Service Content: Drain Cleaning

Related FAQs: faq\_01 faq\_07

Supporting Proof: proof\_02

The binding uses Rule & Form identity.

It does not use Framer collection positions or array indexes as meaning.

Ordering is contextual

A content entity does not need one global order.

Drain Cleaning might be:

homepage priority: 2

while appearing:

alphabetically

on a services index.

Ordering belongs to the Website Engine or the specific relationship being resolved.

Do not write one generic:

sort\_order = 2

onto Service Content and expect it to represent every website context.

Field semantics

Content fields must have defined meaning.

A field is not merely:

string

because the storage system accepts strings.

For each field, the content schema should know whether it is:

required optional

shared across locales localized

plain text formatted content

single reference multiple references

asset reference structured value

This allows validation before provider execution.

Short expression

Short content such as:

title headline button label short summary FAQ question

should remain plain text unless formatting is genuinely required.

Do not encode unnecessary markup into ordinary fields.

Long-form expression

Longer formatted content should use a provider-independent representation.

Framer-native rich-text objects must not become canonical Rule & Form content.

The initial representation should be only as complex as the V1 template requires.

Do not build a general block editor before the product needs one.

References use Rule & Form identity

Relationships between content records are explicit.

Examples:

Service Content ↓ FAQ Content

Service Content ↓ Asset

Location Content ↓ Service Content

Project Content ↓ Service Content

References use stable Rule & Form identifiers.

Avoid duplicating full nested content merely because it is convenient for one rendering path.

Service Content

faq\_ids: faq\_01 faq\_07

is conceptually preferable to embedding two independent copies of those FAQs if the FAQs are intended to remain shared.

No orphan references

Compilation fails when a required reference points to content that cannot be resolved.

Service Content ↓ faq\_99

faq\_99 does not exist

↓

validation failure

Do not silently drop required relationships because a downstream provider would tolerate them.

Assets are references

Content records reference Rule & Form-managed Assets.

Service Content ↓ Asset ID ↓ managed Rule & Form Asset

Not:

Service Content ↓ temporary upload URL

and not:

Service Content ↓ Framer image reference

The publishing layer resolves Rule & Form Assets into provider assets.

Asset roles are semantic

A content entity may distinguish:

primary image gallery images team image credential image project image

Do not require templates to infer meaning from array position alone.

assets\[0\]

should not secretly mean:

hero image

unless that contract is explicitly defined.

Localization

One semantic content entity can have multiple approved language expressions.

Service Content

identity: svc.plumbing.drain\_cleaning

↓

English Drain Cleaning

Spanish Limpieza de desagües

The content is related by identity.

It is not two unrelated services.

Shared and localized fields differ

Some values should be localized:

headline summary body FAQ question FAQ answer CTA expression

Some values may legitimately be shared:

license number phone number asset identity service identity structured numeric facts

The schema should know which class a field belongs to.

Missing localization is explicit

Do not silently mix languages because one field is missing.

For a required localized field, the locale policy must say whether the result:

blocks locale publication uses an explicitly permitted shared value is omitted

The publisher does not decide this ad hoc.

Localization & Search Structure owns locale and search policy.

Content State is upstream

Content State owns approved expression and safe-regeneration behavior.

The CMS Content Model consumes it.

Business Record change ↓ Content State evaluation ↓ generated / preserved / locked / approved expression ↓ CMS Content Model

The CMS layer does not independently regenerate content because a field is inconveniently missing.

Locks survive

Suppose an approved service summary has been manually locked.

Business Record changes ↓ safe regeneration ↓ locked summary preserved ↓ Content State Version ↓ Service Content

The CMS Content Model receives the resolved approved value.

It does not know better than the lock.

Overrides, Locks & Safe Regeneration owns that policy.

Website Engine is downstream and adjacent

The Website Engine does not need to duplicate content records.

It binds them into the Site.

CMS Content Model

Service Content FAQ Content Business Profile Content Location Content

Website Engine

Homepage Recipe Service Page Recipe Navigation Section eligibility CTA intent

↓

Website Manifest

For example:

Service Content

Drain Cleaning

does not imply:

Drain Cleaning page exists

The Website Engine determines page eligibility.

Likewise:

FAQ Content exists

does not mean:

FAQ appears everywhere

Placement remains website logic.

Provider publishing

Publishing Maps Rule & Form content fields into the active provider implementation.

Rule & Form

Service Content headline summary image FAQs

↓

Publishing Mapping

↓

Framer

Services collection Name Summary Image FAQ references

The Publishing Mapping is independently versioned.

A Framer field may move or be renamed without redefining Service Content.

Provider shape may differ

Rule & Form may model:

Service Content

while one template requires:

Framer Services item

and another implementation eventually requires:

static generated page data

or:

another CMS provider

That is acceptable.

The CMS Content Model describes Rule & Form content, not Framer storage.

Desired content state

Publishing consumes a complete desired set of managed content.

Conceptually:

frozen Business Record \+ frozen Content State ↓ resolve CMS Content Model ↓ complete desired content set

Suppose:

Version 8

Drain Cleaning Water Heater Repair Repiping

becomes:

Version 9

Drain Cleaning Water Heater Repair Sewer Repair

The desired content projection changes accordingly.

remove desired Repiping content add desired Sewer Repair content

Framer Publishing owns whether a removed provider item is deleted, drafted, or otherwise excluded according to the active publishing mapping.

The CMS Content Model only states what content belongs in desired Rule & Form output.

Stable identity makes updates safe

A content update should resolve to the same logical entity.

Service Content

content\_id: cnt\_service\_drain

service\_id: svc.plumbing.drain\_cleaning

Changing:

headline summary image localized expression

does not create a different service.

This lets publishing reconcile:

existing desired content ↓ new desired content ↓ same identity ↓ update provider representation Slugs remain separate

A service page route may change.

That does not change Service Content identity.

service content identity ≠ page identity ≠ slug ≠ Framer item ID

Keeping these separate prevents routing and provider implementation from contaminating content identity.

Validation

The CMS Content Model is validated before publishing.

Validation should catch at least:

unknown content type invalid content identity missing required field invalid source reference orphan relationship invalid asset reference unsupported field shape required localization missing duplicate identity content incompatible with schema version

Validation of business truth remains upstream.

For example:

claim: 24/7 Emergency Service

being factually allowed belongs to Business Record and business-rule validation.

The CMS validator verifies that the approved content record is structurally valid for publishing.

Do not make every layer independently re-decide whether the business claim is true.

Schema versioning

The CMS Content Model has its own schema version.

That version is not:

Business Record version Content State version Vertical Blueprint version Template version Publishing Mapping version

These concepts evolve independently.

Conceptually:

Business Record Version 18

Content State Version 11

CMS Content Schema 3

Template Version 5

Publishing Mapping 4

The Publishing Run freezes the versions required to reproduce its output.

Schema migration

If CMS Content Schema v4 changes the representation of FAQ relationships, that does not imply business truth changed.

The system may deterministically interpret or rebuild the derived CMS content projection from its authoritative frozen sources.

The CMS projection is derivative.

There is no reason to manufacture a Business Record revision merely because a publishing-content schema improved.

Persistence

V1 does not require every resolved CMS Content record to become a permanently editable first-class table.

There are two legitimate implementation patterns:

derive during compilation

or:

persist a resolved projection for inspection / reuse

The repository should choose the simpler implementation that still supports:

deterministic publishing validation debugging Workbench inspection stable provider mappings where required

If persisted, the content projection remains reproducible from its authoritative inputs.

Persistence does not convert a projection into canonical truth.

Do not create a second content administration system

Avoid:

Business Record ↓ Content State ↓ CMS Content table

then

staff manually edits CMS Content table

That creates conflicting authorities.

Managed edits enter through Business Record or Content State according to what is actually being changed.

Workbench

Workbench should make the content relationship inspectable.

For a Service Content record:

SERVICE CONTENT

Identity svc.plumbing.drain\_cleaning

Business fact source Business Record Version 18

Approved expression source Content State Version 11

English complete

Spanish complete

Referenced assets 3

Used by Homepage Services page Drain Cleaning page

Provider mapping Framer Services item

Workbench should also distinguish:

SOURCE VALUE APPROVED CONTENT CMS CONTENT RECORD LIVE PROVIDER VALUE

That separation makes debugging possible.

If a live website contains the wrong service summary, we should be able to determine whether the error originated in:

Business Record Content State CMS content resolution Website binding Publishing Mapping provider divergence

rather than guessing.

The CMS Content Model is working when:

A Service Content record can be resolved without knowing a Framer collection ID. A Service Content record references stable service identity rather than visible service wording alone. A legitimate custom Business service can receive content without being assigned a false canonical service. Changing approved wording does not change service identity. Changing a page slug does not change content identity. The same Service Content can be bound to multiple website surfaces without duplicating canonical content. The Website Engine decides where content appears rather than the content record deciding its own page placement. Missing optional content can be omitted according to Website Engine rules. Missing required content is detected before provider mutation. FAQ relationships use stable Rule & Form identity. Asset relationships use Rule & Form Asset identity. Temporary upload URLs and Framer asset IDs do not become canonical asset identity. English and Spanish expression remain tied to the same semantic content entity. Missing required Spanish expression cannot silently produce mixed-language output. Content State locks survive CMS content resolution. A Business Record fact change can alter the desired CMS projection without directly writing Framer. Removing an offered service can remove its desired Service Content from the Site without manually locating provider records. Publishing Mapping can change without changing what Service Content means. A Framer collection field can be renamed without rewriting the Business Record or Content State. Invalid references fail before publishing. Workbench can trace a live content value back through CMS Content, Content State, and Business Record where applicable. Persisting a CMS projection does not make it independently editable canonical truth. The demo uses the same CMS Content Model as a real customer Site. Foundation rules The CMS Content Model is a provider-independent website-content contract. It is not the Business Record, Content State, Website Manifest, or provider CMS schema. Business Record owns facts. Content State owns approved expression. The CMS Content Model packages approved expression into typed reusable content records. The Website Engine decides page and section placement. Content identity is Rule & Form identity. Provider IDs never replace it. Visible labels, slugs, array positions, and provider item IDs are not semantic content identity. Known services remain linked through stable canonical service IDs. Legitimate custom content remains representable without false normalization. Reusable content records are separate from pages that consume them. Ordering is contextual unless the domain explicitly defines a global order. Relationships between content records use stable Rule & Form references. Assets are referenced through Rule & Form Asset identity. Localized expressions remain attached to the same semantic content identity. The schema distinguishes localized fields from deliberately shared fields. Required localization never silently falls back to the wrong language. Content generation and approval happen upstream in Content State, not during provider publishing. Content State locks and approved overrides survive CMS resolution. The CMS Content Model expresses desired content, not current provider reality. Publishing Mapping translates Rule & Form content into provider-specific CMS structures. Provider schema changes do not redefine Rule & Form content meaning. CMS Content Schema versioning is separate from Business Record, Content State, Template, Blueprint, and Publishing Mapping versions. Validation happens before provider mutation. A persisted CMS projection remains derivative and recomputable. Rule & Form does not create another editable source of truth merely because publishing needs structured content. V1 implements only the content types the first real Plumbing Site requires. The same model serves demo and production.

The CMS Content Model should make publishing boring.

By the time Framer sees a content record, Rule & Form should already know what it is, what it means, which approved expression belongs to it, and which Site structures are allowed to use it.

Own the meaning. Structure the content. Map the provider last.
