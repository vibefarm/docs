## title: "Business Record"<br />description: "The canonical, versioned representation of what Rule & Form is allowed to believe about a business, how those facts change, and how every downstream system consumes them safely."

The Business Record is what Rule & Form is allowed to believe about a business.

It holds canonical business facts once, under stable identity, with enough history to know what changed and which version every downstream system used.

```text
Business
    ↓
Business Record
    ↓
Website
Content
Provisioning
Provider configuration
Business Hub
Search structure
Future systems
```

One business is represented once.

## What belongs here

The Business Record contains facts and durable business-level choices that should remain true regardless of which product surface or provider consumes them.

Examples include:

```text
Identity
Business name
Legal name where required
Business type
Years / founding information

Contact
Phone
Email
Website
Business address

Services
Offered services
Custom services
Service priorities
Specialties
Customer types

Geography
Service areas
Locations

Availability
Business hours
Emergency availability
Relevant scheduling facts

Proof
Licenses
Credentials
Certifications
Warranties
Business attributes

Commercial facts
Offers
Financing availability

Languages
Supported customer languages

Assets
Logo
Team photos
Project photos
Vehicles
Credentials

Business-level preferences
Relevant contact or operating preferences
```

This is a semantic boundary, not a requirement that every value live in one database row or JSON object.

The physical schema can use structured payloads, related tables, or both where identity, constraints, querying, or lifecycle justify it.

The authority does not change.

## What does not belong here

The Business Record is not a container for every piece of customer state.

It does not own:

```text
Generated marketing copy
Approved website wording
Manual website overrides
Page composition
Design direction
Template assignment

Leads
Customers
Jobs
Messages
Calls
Appointments

Provider credentials
Provider IDs
Provider transport state

Capability State
Provisioning Runs
Publishing Runs
Published Snapshots

Audit history
System Events
```

Those objects may reference Business Record facts.

They do not become Business Record facts themselves.

```text
Business Record
    what is true

Content State
    what Rule & Form has approved to say

Operational domain
    what is happening

Capability State
    what currently works

Published Snapshot
    exactly what state produced a publication
```

**Do not make one object answer five different questions.**

## One logical record, versioned state

Each Business has one logical Business Record.

That record changes through immutable versions.

```text
Business
    ↓
Business Record
    ├── Version 1
    ├── Version 2
    ├── Version 3
    └── Version 4  ← current
```

The root establishes stable identity.

The versions preserve state through time.

Conceptually:

```text
Business Record

id
organization_id
business_id
current_version_id
```

```text
Business Record Version

id
business_record_id

version
schema_version

canonical state

created_at
created_by
change context
```

The exact physical representation may differ.

The invariants do not.

### Historical versions do not change

Once Version 12 exists, Version 12 remains the state Version 12 represented.

A later correction creates Version 13.

```text
Version 12
24/7 emergency = true

correction
        ↓

Version 13
24/7 emergency = false
```

Version 12 is not rewritten to make history cleaner.

If a Site was published from Version 12, the Published Snapshot can still identify exactly what canonical state that publication used.

### Current state is a pointer

The current Business Record is the version referenced by `current_version_id`.

```text
Business Record
    current_version_id
          ↓
    Version 18
```

Changing current state means creating a valid new version and advancing that pointer atomically.

Historical consumers continue to reference the version they already pinned.

---

## Record version and schema version are different

Two forms of versioning exist.

```text
Record version
    revision of one Business's facts

Schema version
    structure and interpretation of Business Record data
```

Example:

```text
Johnson Plumbing

Record Version 18
Schema Version 3
```

Another Business may be:

```text
Smith Plumbing

Record Version 6
Schema Version 3
```

A schema migration does not mean the customer changed their business.

A customer changing their hours does not mean the Business Record schema changed.

**Never use one counter for both concepts.**

## Stable identity inside the record

Canonical concepts use stable identity wherever Rule & Form already knows the domain.

A known plumbing service is not stored only as visible text.

```text
service_id
svc.plumbing.drain_cleaning
```

The display label can change later.

The identity remains.

```text
svc.plumbing.drain_cleaning

v1
Drain Cleaning

v2
Drain & Clog Cleaning
```

The Business still offers the same canonical service.

### Custom facts remain representable

Reality will not always fit the current Vertical Blueprint.

A custom service stays explicit.

```text
service_id
null

custom_label
Backflow Testing & Certification
```

Do not force an outlier into the nearest canonical category merely to avoid a custom value.

If the same custom concept appears repeatedly across businesses, it may become a candidate for a future Vertical Blueprint version.

That is a taxonomy decision.

It does not retroactively change historical business truth.

### Array position is not identity

Do not make meaning depend on:

```text
services[3]
photos[0]
locations[2]
```

where the item itself has durable meaning.

Items that need to survive reordering, editing, comparison, or downstream mapping use stable identity.

Ordering is stored separately when ordering itself has meaning.

## Offering and priority are different facts

A Business may offer many services and actively prefer more work in only a few.

```text
Offered

Drain Cleaning
Water Heater Repair
Repiping
Sewer Repair
Leak Detection
Fixtures
```

```text
Priority

1. Sewer Repair
2. Repiping
3. Water Heater Replacement
```

Do not infer priority from whether a service is present.

Do not infer it from arbitrary UI order.

These facts may later influence different systems.

```text
offered service
    ↓
service eligibility

priority
    ↓
website prominence
future acquisition logic
```

The Business Record preserves the distinction.

---

## Unknown is not false

Missing information must not silently become a negative claim.

These states mean different things:

```text
financing = true
    confirmed offered

financing = false
    confirmed not offered

financing = unknown / absent
    Rule & Form does not know yet
```

Likewise:

```text
24/7 emergency = false
```

is not equivalent to:

```text
24/7 emergency was never asked
```

This matters because downstream systems make decisions from the Business Record.

**Absence of evidence is not a business fact.**

Where the distinction matters, the schema preserves it explicitly.

---

## Valid does not mean complete

A Business Record can exist before Rule & Form knows every optional fact about the Business.

It cannot contain invalid or unconfirmed claims merely to appear complete.

```text
Known and confirmed
    canonical

Unknown
    remains unknown

Suggested
    not canonical

Contradictory
    rejected or reviewed
```

Different systems require different subsets of the record.

```text
Website
    requires one set of facts

A2P registration
    requires another

Booking
    requires another

Google Business Profile
    requires another
```

A capability determines whether the facts it needs are present.

The Business Record does not have one global `complete = true` flag that pretends every consumer has identical requirements.

This is what allows progressive provisioning.

---

## Authority

Canonical state changes only through an authoritative Rule & Form write path.

Possible actors include:

```text
Authorized customer
Authorized Rule & Form staff
Controlled system operation using already-authoritative state
```

AI is not an authoritative actor.

A public website is not an authoritative actor.

A directory listing is not an authoritative actor.

A provider webhook is not an authoritative actor for canonical business facts.

### Suggestions are not facts

Structured Intake, imports, AI, and staff research may produce provisional information.

```text
Observed
Suggested
Imported
        ↓
review / confirmation
        ↓
Confirmed
        ↓
Business Record
```

Example:

```text
Existing website says
"24-hour plumbing"

        ↓

Imported suggestion
24/7 Emergency

        ↓

customer confirms
        ↓

canonical Business Record fact
```

Without the confirmation required by the field's authority rule, it remains provisional.

### Providers do not silently rewrite business truth

Suppose HighLevel contains a different business phone number from the Business Record.

That disagreement does not make HighLevel correct.

```text
Business Record
(325) 555-1000

HighLevel
(325) 555-2000

        ↓

drift / discrepancy
```

Provider state may trigger reconciliation or review.

It does not silently take canonical ownership.

**One field. One authority.**

## Provenance

Rule & Form should be able to explain how a canonical fact entered the system.

Useful provenance may include:

```text
source type
source reference
actor
confirmation method
timestamp
change reason
```

Not every scalar field must physically carry a large provenance object.

The implementation can attach provenance at the mutation, version, field, or related-record level where appropriate.

The requirement is simpler:

**For a material business claim, we should be able to determine why Rule & Form believes it.**

Examples:

```text
Licensed
    confirmed by customer during intake

License number
    supplied by customer

Spanish service
    confirmed during intake

Business hours
    changed by Rule & Form staff from customer request
```

This becomes particularly important for claims that affect public content, provider registration, or customer expectations.

## The write path

Every canonical mutation follows the same conceptual path.

```text
proposed change
      ↓
resolve tenant + Business
      ↓
authorize actor
      ↓
validate input
      ↓
load current version
      ↓
apply intended change
      ↓
run domain validation
      ↓
create new version
      ↓
advance current pointer
      ↓
record change / emit required event
      ↓
commit
```

Downstream work begins only after the canonical change exists durably.

### No direct provider mutation

Avoid:

```text
intake service selected
        ↓
write Framer CMS
```

Avoid:

```text
staff edits HighLevel
        ↓
Business Record silently changes
```

Prefer:

```text
authorized business change
        ↓
Business Record Version 19
        ↓
downstream systems react
```

The Business Record is the handoff between understanding the business and operating systems that serve it.

---

## Concurrency

Two edits can begin from the same current version.

They must not silently overwrite each other.

```text
Editor A
loads Version 18

Editor B
loads Version 18

Editor A
commits Version 19

Editor B
tries to commit from Version 18
```

The second write detects that its base is stale.

It must reload, reconcile, or explicitly merge the intended change.

It does not blindly replace Version 19.

The exact implementation may use optimistic concurrency, version checks, or equivalent transaction controls.

The invariant is:

**A later write must not erase an intervening canonical change without knowing that change occurred.**

## Validation

A proposed Business Record version must pass validation before becoming current.

Validation occurs at several levels.

### Schema validation

The record conforms to the Business Record schema version.

Examples:

```text
valid field shape
valid canonical identifier
valid structured address
valid contact representation
valid collection shape
```

### Field validation

Individual values are structurally valid.

Examples:

```text
phone
email
URL
date
numeric value
supported asset reference
```

### Relationship validation

Related facts do not contradict the model.

Examples:

```text
priority service
    must also be offered

custom service
    must not pretend to have a canonical service ID

asset reference
    must resolve to a valid Rule & Form Asset
```

### Business-rule validation

Some combinations require product rules.

Examples:

```text
24/7 Emergency
    may require an appropriate contact path

Spanish enabled
    may introduce downstream localization requirements
```

The common rule set belongs to **Validation & Business Rules**.

The Business Record write path invokes it.

The UI does not become a second authority for the same rule.

---

## Change semantics

A new version represents a meaningful change in canonical state.

Examples:

```text
service added
service removed
phone changed
hours changed
credential confirmed
service area added
offer ended
language added
priority reordered
```

A request that produces no semantic change does not need to manufacture history merely because a save button was clicked.

### Removing a fact preserves history

Suppose Version 14 includes Sewer Repair.

Version 15 removes it.

```text
Version 14
Sewer Repair offered

Version 15
Sewer Repair absent / no longer offered
```

Historical Version 14 remains recoverable.

Removal changes current truth.

It does not erase prior truth.

## Downstream consumption

Ordinary application reads may use the current Business Record Version.

Long-running or reproducible operations pin a specific version.

```text
current product view
        ↓
current Business Record Version
```

```text
Publishing Run
        ↓
Business Record Version 18
        ↓
same version for entire run
```

A Publishing Run does not begin on Version 18 and silently finish using Version 19 because the customer edited their hours halfway through.

The new canonical state can drive the next run.

### Content State

The Business Record supplies facts.

Content State owns approved expression.

```text
Business Record

family_owned = true
founded_year = 2012

        ↓

Content State

"Family-owned plumbing serving Abilene since 2012."
```

Editing that sentence does not alter `family_owned` or `founded_year`.

Changing `founded_year` does not give downstream generation permission to overwrite a locked approved sentence.

The two systems have different authority.

### Website and CMS

The Website Engine and CMS Content Model derive website state from a pinned Business Record Version plus approved Content State.

They do not become alternative business databases.

### Providers

Providers receive only the Business Record facts required for their role.

```text
Business Record
      ↓
explicit projection
      ↓
provider configuration
```

Do not mirror the entire Business Record into HighLevel, Framer, or another vendor because it is convenient.

A projection is not another source of truth.

---

## Schema evolution

The Business Record schema will improve as Rule & Form learns more about Plumbing and later verticals.

A schema change must preserve interpretability of historical versions.

```text
Schema v1
    ↓
Schema v2
    ↓
Schema v3
```

Possible changes include:

```text
new optional field
new canonical category
stronger validation
new structured representation
deprecated field
new relationship
```

Schema evolution does not rewrite historical meaning silently.

If old state needs migration, that migration is explicit and reproducible.

A Published Snapshot that references an older Business Record Version must remain explainable after the current schema advances.

## V1 boundary

V1 implements the Business Record deeply enough to represent one real Plumbing business and drive the complete product loop. V1 scope

### Required

V1 requires:

- one logical Business Record per Business
- immutable Business Record Versions
- independent schema version
- atomic current-version pointer
- tenant ownership through Organization and Business
- one authorized write path
- stable canonical IDs for known Plumbing concepts
- preserved custom values for legitimate exceptions
- explicit offered-versus-priority distinction
- correct unknown-versus-false semantics where required
- identity and contact facts
- Plumbing services and specialties required by the product
- service areas and locations required by the product
- hours and availability required by the product
- credentials and proof required by the first Site
- offers and financing facts required by the first Site
- language facts required for English and Spanish output
- Rule & Form Asset references required by the first Site
- validation before a version becomes current
- concurrency protection
- enough provenance to explain material canonical claims
- current-version reads
- pinned-version reads for publishing and other reproducible work
- change history visible in Workbench
- deterministic demo fixture using the same model

### Deferred

V1 does not require:

- every possible Plumbing fact
- every future trade schema
- arbitrary customer-defined fields
- AI-created canonical business facts
- autonomous taxonomy expansion
- a universal ontology for every local business
- perfect historical field-level provenance for every trivial value
- storing every Business Record field as a relational column
- storing the entire record in one document
- general-purpose schema editing UI
- provider-specific fields in the canonical model merely because one provider exposes them

**Model the business we need to serve now without making the first schema the last schema.**

## Acceptance conditions

The Business Record is working when:

- A Business has one logical Business Record with identifiable immutable versions.
- A canonical change creates a new version rather than rewriting historical state.
- Record version and schema version remain independent.
- A Publishing Run can pin one Business Record Version and use it for the entire run.
- A historical Published Snapshot can still resolve the Business Record Version it used.
- A known Plumbing service is represented by stable canonical identity rather than visible wording alone.
- A legitimate custom service can remain custom without false normalization.
- A service can be offered without being prioritized.
- Unknown information cannot silently become `false`.
- AI or imported information cannot become canonical merely because it looks plausible.
- A provider webhook cannot silently overwrite a canonical business fact.
- Two concurrent edits cannot unknowingly erase one another.
- Invalid state cannot become the current Business Record Version.
- Removing a service changes current truth without erasing the historical version that contained it.
- Changing approved website copy does not change canonical business facts.
- Changing a canonical business fact does not directly mutate Framer or HighLevel.
- Downstream systems can consume current state without maintaining competing copies of the same fact.
- Workbench can explain a material fact, the version containing it, and how it entered canonical state.
- Demo and production use the same Business Record model.

2. **One Business has one logical Business Record.**
3. **Canonical changes create immutable Business Record Versions.**
4. **Record version and schema version are different concepts.**
5. **Historical versions are never rewritten to make current state cleaner.**
6. **Known domain concepts use stable canonical identity.**
7. **Legitimate exceptions remain representable without false normalization.**
8. **Offering something and prioritizing it are separate facts.**
9. **Unknown is not false.**
10. **Valid state does not imply every possible field is complete.**
11. **Canonical state enters through one authorized write path.**
12. **AI, imports, public sources, and provider state do not silently create canonical business facts.**
13. **Every canonical field has one authority.**
14. **Material canonical claims retain enough provenance to explain why Rule & Form believes them.**
15. **Concurrent writes cannot silently erase intervening canonical changes.**
16. **A proposed version must pass schema and domain validation before becoming current.**
17. **Removing current truth does not erase historical truth.**
18. **Content State owns approved expression, not the Business Record.**
19. **Operational activity does not belong in the Business Record.**
20. **Provider IDs, credentials, and runtime state do not belong in the Business Record.**
21. **Provider projections consume canonical state. They do not become competing truth.**
22. **Long-running reproducible work pins a specific Business Record Version.**
23. **Schema evolution preserves the interpretability of historical state.**
24. **The same Business Record model serves demo and production.**

Everything downstream gets easier when the business itself is settled first.

**Represent it once. Version the truth. Make everything else consume it.**
