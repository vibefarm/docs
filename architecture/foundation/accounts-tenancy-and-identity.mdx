Authentication proves who is acting.

Authorization determines what that actor may do.

Tenancy determines which customer's data the action may reach.

Those are separate concerns, and a login is never by itself permission to access a business.

<Callout icon="terminal" color="#8B5CF6">
  **For implementation** — this page owns the authorization and tenant-safety contract. [Domain Model & Database Schema](/architecture/foundation/domain-model-and-database-schema) owns how the schema enforces it. Subsystem pages own behavior inside their domains. Inspect the repository and the relevant Build Spec page before changing a tenant-owned code path.
</Callout>

## The identity model

```text
User
  ↓
Membership
  ↓
Organization          ← tenant boundary
  ↓
Business              ← represented company
  ↓
Business data
```

| Object | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **User** | An authenticated human who can access the Rule & Form application |
| **Organization** | The tenant boundary. All customer-owned data resolves beneath one Organization |
| **Membership** | The relationship between a User and an Organization. Permissions attach here |
| **Business** | The contractor or company Rule & Form represents and operates for |

These remain distinct even when V1 commonly has one User, one Organization, and one Business.

**The simple case must not collapse the model.**

<Accordion title="Canonical terminology — Organization, not account">
  **Organization** is the Rule & Form domain term for the tenant boundary.

  Authentication or framework code may use terms such as `account` internally. Those implementation names do not create a second domain concept.

  Build Spec pages, domain code, and schema naming converge on **Organization** unless an external library contract requires otherwise.

  [Naming, IDs & Conventions](/architecture/foundation/naming-ids-and-conventions) is the enforcement point.
</Accordion>

### Organization and Business

Organization answers **whose account is this**. Business answers **which business are we representing**.

They are not interchangeable. An Organization can eventually own more than one Business without redefining user identity or tenant ownership. V1 may expose only one Business per Organization <Badge color="blue" size="sm" stroke>V1 scope</Badge>, but the domain must not require that assumption forever.

```text
Organization
  ↓
Business
  ├── Business Record
  ├── Site(s)
  ├── operational records
  ├── integrations
  └── capabilities
```

Business is also not a second tenant key. A Business always resolves to its owning Organization, and authority is verified against that Organization before a business-scoped operation runs.

```text
businessId
    ↓
owning Organization
    ↓
verify authority
    ↓
scoped operation
```

**Knowing a Business ID is not authorization to act on it.**

This is separate from the one-Business-to-many-Sites relationship defined in the [domain model](/architecture/foundation/domain-model-and-database-schema).

### Users and Memberships

A User represents application identity. Authentication state, sessions, credential mechanisms, and identity verification belong to the identity system. Business facts do not.

```text
Login email
    owner@example.com

Business contact email
    service@johnsonplumbing.com
```

Those values may match. They mean different things.

Access to an Organization exists through Membership or explicit internal authority. Permissions attach to the relationship between the actor and the Organization — they do not become global properties of the User.

```text
User
  ├── Membership → Johnson Plumbing
  └── Membership → Second Organization
```

A User being an administrator in one Organization grants no authority in another.

## Authority and tenant context

Better Auth currently provides application authentication and session management. Rule & Form owns the authorization and tenant-safety requirements around it.

The authentication library can change without changing **User · Organization · Membership · Business · tenant ownership**.

Authentication establishes the actor. It does not establish tenant scope.

### Resolving the tenant

Every customer-owned request resolves an authorized tenant before accessing customer data.

```text
Session
    ↓
Authenticated User
    ↓
Requested Organization
    ↓
Verify Membership / authority
    ↓
Authorized tenant context
    ↓
Business operation
```

<Callout icon="lock" color="#E2E8F0">
  An Organization or Business identifier supplied by the client identifies a **requested resource**. It is never proof of permission.
</Callout>

Authority is verified on every request. A session that outlives a Membership does not retain the access that Membership previously granted.

## Tenant isolation

This page owns the tenant-isolation contract.

Every operation involving tenant-owned data carries the relevant Organization context into the data-access boundary. Business-scoped operations also prove that the Business belongs to that authorized Organization.

```text
getLeads(organizationId, ...)
getBusinessRecord(organizationId, businessId)
getSite(organizationId, businessId, siteId)
```

The exact function signatures may differ. The invariant does not.

Before tenant-owned data can be read or changed:

1. the actor is authenticated where required,
2. authority over the Organization is verified,
3. Business ownership is verified where applicable,
4. the database operation is scoped to that tenant.

We do not:

- retrieve global customer data and filter afterward
- trust a tenant identifier because the client supplied it
- treat knowledge of a Business or object ID as authorization
- rely on UI visibility as access control
- assume authenticated means authorized

### Database enforcement

Explicit application scoping is the primary tenant-isolation mechanism. Tenant-owned queries apply their Organization boundary at the database query itself.

**Row Level Security is not the sole guarantee.**

<Accordion title="Why RLS cannot be the primary mechanism">
  Privileged server processes, background work, integrations, and internal operations may execute outside ordinary user-scoped database policies. Tenant scope therefore remains mandatory in application data access.

  Database policies may provide defense in depth where useful. They do not replace explicit tenant scoping.
</Accordion>

Three questions, three answers:

| Question | Answered by |
| --- | --- |
| Who is acting? | Authentication |
| May they perform this action? | Authorization |
| Which Organization's data may the action reach? | Tenant scope |

### Public demo

The public demo resolves only to a dedicated Organization containing synthetic Business data. It may run the same production application code, but it cannot switch tenant context into a customer Organization.

Where the demo exercises external systems, it uses dedicated demo resources or controlled simulation.

<Callout icon="lock" color="#E2E8F0">
  A public demo action must be incapable of publishing over, messaging through, modifying, or retrieving a real customer account.
</Callout>

## Non-interactive tenant resolution

Requests without a customer session obey the same boundary. They resolve their tenant from trusted server-side state rather than from whatever identifier happens to be in hand.

### Background work

Background jobs have no interactive user session, so their tenant context must be explicit.

```text
Provisioning Run
    ↓
Organization
    ↓
Business
    ↓
Scoped records
    ↓
Provider command
```

Scheduled work, reconciliation, publishing, retries, and asynchronous commands obey the same boundary as interactive requests.

**A background process does not discover its tenant by searching globally for a convenient matching record.**

### Webhooks

Provider webhooks arrive without a Rule & Form customer session. Their tenant is resolved through a trusted <Tooltip tip="Rule & Form's configured relationship with an external provider resource, owned by an Organization.">Integration Connection</Tooltip> or <Tooltip tip="The association between a Rule & Form object and its counterpart in a connected provider.">Provider Mapping</Tooltip>.

```text
Signed provider event
    ↓
Verify signature            → did the provider send this?
    ↓
Provider Mapping
    ↓
Integration Connection
    ↓
Organization / Business     → which tenant owns it?
    ↓
Record event
    ↓
Process inside tenant scope
```

Signature verification and provider mapping answer different questions. Both are required.

A provider identifier is not authorization by itself.

### Internal Rule & Form access

Rule & Form requires privileged access to operate a managed product. Staff do not use customer passwords or customer authentication credentials.

An internal actor enters an explicit tenant context before accessing customer-owned data.

```text
Internal User
    ↓
Internal authority
    ↓
Selected Organization
    ↓
Tenant-scoped operation
```

Privileged access does not remove the tenant boundary. It grants an authorized actor the ability to enter one.

Sensitive internal actions are auditable — changes to canonical business data, overrides, publishing, integration configuration, and actions performed on behalf of a customer.

<Callout icon="terminal" color="#8B5CF6">
  **V1 scope** — the initial product needs one internal authority level, explicit tenant selection, and an audit record for sensitive actions. A full internal role matrix is not required. If impersonation is introduced, it is treated as privileged internal access with explicit audit history.
</Callout>

## Identities that are not Rule & Form identities

### Website visitors

A homeowner visiting a contractor website is not a Rule & Form User. A form submission, call, text, or booking creates or maps to customer-acquisition records.

```text
Website visitor
    ↓
Customer
    ↓
Lead / Job inquiry
```

It does not create an application account. Authentication identity and contractor-customer identity remain separate domains.

### Provider identities

A HighLevel contact, Google account, Stripe customer, Framer project, or other provider object does not become a Rule & Form User, Organization, or Business.

```text
Rule & Form Business
    ↓
Integration Connection
    ↓
Provider Resource
```

Provider credentials prove access to an external resource. They do not establish ownership inside Rule & Form.

## Roles and lifecycle

### Customer roles

The architecture supports roles and permissions. V1 does not require broad customer RBAC. <Badge color="blue" size="sm" stroke>V1 scope</Badge>

```text
User
    ↓
Membership
    ↓
Role / Permission
    ↓
Organization
```

Multiple customer employees, invitations, granular permissions, and richer account administration are added when observed product requirements justify them. Future permissions attach to Memberships and explicit actions, and never weaken tenant isolation.

### Customer authority and internal authority

Customer authority and Rule & Form authority are separate domains.

A customer administrator may administer their Organization — that grants no Rule & Form internal privilege. A Rule & Form operator may have controlled access across Organizations — that does not make the operator a customer member of every Organization.

Support tooling must not contaminate customer identity.

### Account lifecycle

Creating an account establishes identity and tenant ownership before business data is created underneath it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create or authorize the User">
    Application identity only.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the Organization">
    The tenant boundary now exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the Membership">
    Authority attaches to the relationship.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the Business">
    The represented company, beneath the tenant.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Begin intake">
    Business data is written under an established owner.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Closing an account is not equivalent to deleting its User. Identity lifecycle, business-data lifecycle, provider disconnection, retention, export, and offboarding remain separate concerns, and detailed behavior belongs in the later operating and security specifications.

## Security boundary tests

Tenant isolation is a release requirement, not an architectural assumption.

V1 must prove:

- Tenant A cannot read Tenant B data.
- Tenant A cannot write Tenant B data.
- A Business ID owned by Tenant B is rejected under Tenant A's authority.
- Changing a Business, Site, Lead, or other object ID cannot cross the tenant boundary.
- Background jobs remain scoped to their declared Organization.
- Webhook mappings resolve to one known Organization before processing.
- The public demo cannot access customer data.

When privileged internal access ships, its tenant selection and sensitive actions are added to the same test surface.

These tests verify behavior rather than trusting framework configuration.

<Accordion title="What this page does not decide">
  - final customer and internal role matrices

  - impersonation UX
  - detailed offboarding and retention behavior

  Those decisions belong to later product and security passes. The boundary is already fixed: **identity, Membership, Organization, Business, customer identity, and provider identity are separate concepts.**
</Accordion>

## Foundation rules

1. **Authentication, authorization, and tenancy are separate concerns.**
2. **Organization is the tenant boundary.**
3. **Business belongs to an Organization. It is not a second tenant boundary.**
4. **Knowing an Organization, Business, or object ID does not grant authority over it.**
5. **Users reach Organizations through explicit Membership or explicit internal authority.**
6. **Every tenant-owned operation carries explicit Organization scope.**
7. **Business-scoped operations verify Business ownership inside the authorized Organization.**
8. **Background jobs and webhooks obey the same tenant boundary as interactive requests.**
9. **Internal privilege never removes explicit tenant context.**
10. **Customer roles and Rule & Form internal roles remain separate.**
11. **Website visitors and contractor customers are not application Users.**
12. **Provider identities and credentials never become Rule & Form identity.**
13. **Cross-tenant isolation is tested directly.**
14. **Authority is revalidated per request.**
15. **V1 stays narrow without collapsing distinctions the later product will need.**

**Know the actor. Resolve the tenant. Verify the authority. Then touch the data.**
