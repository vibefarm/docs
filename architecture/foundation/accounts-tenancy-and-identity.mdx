---
title: "Accounts, Tenancy & Identity"
description: "How Rule & Form separates authentication, authorization, tenancy, business identity, and internal authority while enforcing Organization isolation across customer and system access."
---

Authentication proves who is acting.

Authorization determines what that actor may do.

Tenancy determines which customer's data the action may reach.

Those are separate concerns, and a login is never by itself permission to access a Business.

 \<Callout icon="terminal" color="#8B5CF6"\> **For implementation** — Supabase Auth owns authentication mechanics. Rule & Form owns Membership, authorization, Organization tenancy, Business ownership, and internal authority. Customer-scoped access uses both explicit Organization scope and PostgreSQL Row Level Security. Privileged system access may bypass ordinary RLS technically, but it never bypasses Rule & Form tenant resolution. \</Callout\> 

## The identity model

```text
```

```text
Supabase Auth
      ↓
User
      ↓
Membership
      ↓
Organization          ← tenant boundary
      ↓
Business              ← represented company
      ↓
Business-owned state
```

| Object | What it is |
| :-- | :-- |
| **User** | An authenticated human recognized by the Rule & Form application |
| **Membership** | The relationship between a User and an Organization. Customer authority attaches here |
| **Organization** | The tenant boundary. Customer-owned state resolves beneath one Organization |
| **Business** | The contractor or company Rule & Form represents and operates for |

These remain distinct even when V1 commonly has one User, one Organization, and one Business.

**The simple case must not collapse the model.**

<br />\<Accordion title="Canonical terminology — Organization, not account"\><br />  **Organization** is the Rule & Form domain term for the tenant boundary.<br />

Authentication, framework, or provider code may use terms such as `account`, `workspace`, or `team`. Those implementation names do not create another Rule & Form domain concept.

Build Spec pages, domain code, schema naming, and operational tooling converge on **Organization** unless an external contract requires otherwise.

[Naming, IDs & Conventions](/architecture/foundation/naming-ids-and-conventions) is the enforcement point.<br /><br />\</Accordion\>

### Organization and Business

Organization answers **whose Rule & Form tenant is this**.

Business answers **which real company are we representing**.

They are not interchangeable.

```text
```

```text
Organization
    ↓
Business
    ├── Business Record
    ├── Site(s)
    ├── Customers / Leads / Jobs
    ├── Conversations / Appointments
    ├── integrations
    └── capabilities
```

An Organization can eventually own more than one Business without redefining identity or tenancy.

V1 may expose only one Business per Organization. \<Badge color="blue" size="sm" stroke\>V1 scope\</Badge\>

The domain must not require that assumption forever.

Business is not a second tenant boundary.

```text
```

```text
businessId
    ↓
owning Organization
    ↓
verify authority
    ↓
business-scoped operation
```

**Knowing a Business ID is not authorization to act on it.**

### Users and Memberships

A User represents application identity.

Authentication state, sessions, credential mechanisms, and sign-in flows belong to the authentication system. Business facts do not.

```text
```

```text
User login email

owner@example.com


Business public email

service@johnsonplumbing.com
```

Those values may happen to match. They mean different things.

Access to an Organization exists through a current Membership or explicit Rule & Form internal authority.

```text
```

```text
User
  ├── Membership → Johnson Plumbing
  └── Membership → Second Organization
```

Authority belongs to the relationship.

A User being an administrator in one Organization grants no authority in another.

## Authentication and authority

Supabase Auth establishes the authenticated User and session.

Rule & Form establishes what that User may reach.

```text
```

```text
Supabase session
        ↓
authenticated User
        ↓
Rule & Form Membership
        ↓
authorized Organization
        ↓
Business operation
```

The authentication provider can change without redefining:

**User · Membership · Organization · Business · tenant ownership**

Authentication establishes the actor.

It does not establish tenant scope.

### Resolving the tenant

Every customer-owned request resolves an authorized Organization before accessing customer data.

```text
```

```text
Session
    ↓
Authenticated User
    ↓
Requested Organization
    ↓
Verify current Membership
    ↓
Authorized tenant context
    ↓
Requested Business
    ↓
Verify Business ownership
    ↓
Business operation
```

<br />\<Callout icon="lock" color="#E2E8F0"\><br />  An Organization, Business, Site, Lead, or other identifier supplied by a client identifies a requested resource. It is never proof of permission.<br />\</Callout\><br />

A valid session that outlives a Membership does not preserve the authority that Membership previously granted.

### Membership remains application authority

Membership is canonical Rule & Form authorization state.

Do not make Organization authority depend solely on data copied into a browser, user-editable profile metadata, or cached session claims.

```text
```

```text
authentication
    identifies the User

Membership
    establishes Organization authority
```

If a Membership is removed:

```text
```

```text
User remains authenticated

but

Organization authority is gone
```

Authentication and authorization remain separate throughout the session lifecycle.

## Tenant isolation

Every tenant-owned operation carries explicit Organization context into the data-access boundary.

Business-scoped operations also prove that the Business belongs to that Organization.

Conceptually:

```text
```

```text
getLeads(organizationId, ...)

getBusinessRecord(
    organizationId,
    businessId
)

getSite(
    organizationId,
    businessId,
    siteId
)
```

The exact function signatures may differ.

The invariant does not.

Before tenant-owned data can be read or changed:

1. <br />the actor is authenticated where required,<br />
2. <br />authority over the Organization is verified,<br />
3. <br />Business ownership is verified where applicable,<br />
4. <br />the database operation remains scoped to that tenant.<br />

We do not:

- <br />retrieve global customer data and filter afterward<br />
- <br />trust a tenant identifier because the client supplied it<br />
- <br />treat knowledge of an object ID as authorization<br />
- <br />rely on UI visibility as access control<br />
- <br />assume authenticated means authorized<br />

## Two-layer containment

Customer-scoped access uses two protections together.

```text
```

```text
APPLICATION

authorized Organization
        ↓
tenant-scoped domain operation

        +

DATABASE

PostgreSQL RLS
        ↓
row-level containment
```

Application scope and RLS protect different failure modes.

### Explicit application scope

Tenant awareness remains part of Rule & Form domain code.

Conceptually:

```text
```

```text
SELECT ...
FROM leads
WHERE organization_id = :authorizedOrganization
```

not:

```text
```

```text
SELECT ...
FROM leads
```

followed by filtering in the frontend.

Business rules and tenant scope are resolved before the result reaches presentation code.

### Row Level Security

Customer-accessible tenant-owned tables use appropriate PostgreSQL Row Level Security policies.

RLS provides a second containment boundary if customer-scoped application code attempts to access rows outside its authorized Organization.

Conceptually:

```text
```

```text
authenticated User
        ↓
current Membership
        ↓
Organization
        ↓
tenant-owned row
```

RLS policy definitions belong in version-controlled database migrations alongside the schema they protect.

[Domain Model & Database Schema](/architecture/foundation/domain-model-and-database-schema) owns the relational enforcement.

### Why both are required

RLS does not remove tenant scope from application code.

Application scope does not make database containment unnecessary.

```text
```

```text
authorization
        +
explicit tenant scope
        +
RLS
        +
relational integrity
        ↓
tenant boundary
```

**Defense in depth does not mean duplicate sources of truth. It means independent containment.**

## Privileged system access

Some Rule & Form operations do not run under an interactive customer session.

Examples include:

```text
```

```text
provider webhooks
background jobs
publishing
provider commands
reconciliation
internal operations
controlled provisioning
```

Those operations may require privileged server-side database access.

Privileged access can bypass ordinary customer RLS enforcement.

That makes tenant resolution more important, not less.

```text
```

```text
trusted system trigger
        ↓
resolve Organization
        ↓
resolve Business where applicable
        ↓
tenant-scoped domain operation
        ↓
privileged database access
```

A privileged credential is not tenant authority.

A Provider Mapping is not tenant authority until its ownership has been resolved.

A Business ID is not tenant authority.

### Privileged access stays narrow

Do not make privileged database access the default path for ordinary application code.

Prefer:

```text
```

```text
CUSTOMER PATH

authenticated User
        ↓
Membership
        ↓
Organization
        ↓
customer-scoped access
        ↓
RLS


SYSTEM PATH

trusted server operation
        ↓
explicit tenant resolution
        ↓
Organization
        ↓
narrow privileged access
```

Privileged Supabase credentials remain server-only and never reach browser code, public environment variables, frontend logs, or demo configuration.

## Non-interactive tenant resolution

Requests without a customer session obey the same ownership graph.

They resolve their tenant from trusted Rule & Form state rather than from whichever identifier happens to be convenient.

### Background work

Background jobs carry explicit tenant context.

```text
```

```text
Provisioning Run
    ↓
Organization
    ↓
Business
    ↓
Scoped records
    ↓
Provider Command
```

The same rule applies to:

```text
```

```text
Publishing Runs
Provider Commands
retries
reconciliation
scheduled work
workflow execution
```

**A background process does not discover its tenant by searching globally for a convenient matching record.**

### Webhooks

Provider webhooks arrive without a Rule & Form customer session.

Their tenant is resolved through a trusted Integration Connection or Provider Mapping.

```text
```

```text
Signed provider event
    ↓
Verify provider authenticity
    ↓
Provider Mapping
    ↓
Integration Connection
    ↓
Organization / Business
    ↓
Record Provider Event
    ↓
Process inside tenant scope
```

Signature verification answers:

**Did the provider send this?**

Provider mapping answers:

**Which Rule & Form tenant owns it?**

Both are required.

A valid provider signature does not grant access to every Rule & Form Organization.

A provider identifier is not tenant authority by itself.

## Internal Rule & Form access

Rule & Form requires privileged access to operate a managed product.

Staff do not use customer passwords or customer authentication credentials.

```text
```

```text
Internal User
    ↓
Internal authority
    ↓
Selected Organization
    ↓
Tenant context
    ↓
Controlled operation
```

Customer authority and Rule & Form internal authority remain separate domains.

A customer administrator having broad authority over their Organization grants no Rule & Form internal privilege.

A Rule & Form operator having authority across Organizations does not make that operator a customer Membership of every Organization.

### V1 internal authority

V1 requires one Rule & Form internal operator authority. \<Badge color="blue" size="sm" stroke\>V1 scope\</Badge\>

That is sufficient for:

- <br />customer setup<br />
- <br />Business Record corrections<br />
- <br />managed changes<br />
- <br />provider configuration<br />
- <br />publishing<br />
- <br />support<br />
- <br />Workbench inspection<br />
- <br />recovery operations<br />

A complex internal role matrix is deferred.

### Internal privilege does not remove tenant context

An internal operator may be authorized to reach many Organizations.

A customer-owned operation still executes inside one explicit tenant context.

```text
```

```text
Internal authority

can enter:
    Organization A
    Organization B
    Organization C

current operation:
    Organization B
```

Sensitive internal actions are auditable.

Audit & Observability owns the audit contract.

## Identities that are not Rule & Form Users

### Website visitors and contractor customers

A homeowner visiting a contractor website is not a Rule & Form User.

```text
```

```text
Website visitor
    ↓
Customer
    ↓
Lead
    ↓
Job / Conversation / Appointment
```

Submitting a form, making a call, sending a text, or booking an appointment does not create an application Membership.

Authentication identity and contractor-customer identity remain separate domains.

### Provider identities

A HighLevel contact, Google account, Stripe object, Framer project, or another provider object does not become a Rule & Form User, Organization, or Business.

```text
```

```text
Rule & Form Business
        ↓
Integration Connection
        ↓
Provider Resource
```

Provider credentials prove Rule & Form can interact with an external resource.

They do not establish ownership inside Rule & Form.

### Authentication fields are not Business facts

Application identity and Business Record data also remain separate.

```text
```

```text
User login email
        ≠
Business public email

User phone
        ≠
Business public phone

User locale
        ≠
Site locale
```

Business Record owns canonical Business facts.

## Customer roles

The architecture supports roles and permissions through Membership.

```text
```

```text
User
    ↓
Membership
    ↓
Role / Permission
    ↓
Organization
```

V1 does not require broad customer RBAC.

The first product may expose one customer authority level:

```text
```

```text
Organization Owner
```

Multiple employees, invitations, organization switching, granular permissions, custom roles, and richer account administration are added when observed product requirements justify them.

Future permissions attach to Memberships and explicit actions.

They never weaken tenant isolation.

## Account creation

Customer account creation establishes tenant ownership before Business data is created underneath it.

<br />\<Steps\><br />  \<Step title="Authenticate the User"\><br />    Supabase Auth establishes application identity.<br />  \</Step\><br />  \<Step title="Create the Organization"\><br />    The tenant boundary now exists.<br />  \</Step\><br />  \<Step title="Create the initial Membership"\><br />    Authority attaches the authenticated User to the Organization.<br />  \</Step\><br />  \<Step title="Create the Business"\><br />    The represented company is created beneath the Organization.<br />  \</Step\><br />  \<Step title="Begin Structured Intake"\><br />    Business state is now collected beneath an established owner.<br />  \</Step\><br />\</Steps\><br />

The initial Organization, Membership, and Business should be created through one controlled onboarding operation where practical.

### Bootstrap authority is narrow

Creating the first Membership presents a necessary bootstrap case:

```text
```

```text
Membership grants authority

but

no Membership exists yet
```

The account-creation path therefore has narrow system authority to create:

```text
```

```text
Organization
initial Membership
initial Business
```

for the authenticated new User.

That exception does not become general permission for authenticated users to create arbitrary Memberships or Organizations.

## Account lifecycle

Authentication lifecycle, Membership lifecycle, Business lifecycle, and customer-data lifecycle remain separate.

```text
```

```text
disable Membership
        ≠
delete User

delete User
        ≠
delete Organization

cancel service
        ≠
delete Business Record

disconnect provider
        ≠
delete audit history
```

V1 needs a controlled way to disable customer access.

Detailed retention, export, deletion, provider disconnection, recovery, and offboarding policy belongs in later security and operating specifications.

The important boundary is already fixed:

**identity lifecycle does not silently destroy Business state.**

## Public demo

The public demo resolves only to a dedicated Organization containing synthetic Business data.

```text
```

```text
Demo identity
        ↓
Demo Organization
        ↓
Demo Business
        ↓
synthetic state
```

It may run the same production application code.

It cannot switch tenant context into a real customer Organization.

Where the demo exercises external systems, it uses dedicated demo provider resources or controlled simulation.

<br />\<Warning\><br />  A public demo action must be incapable of publishing over, messaging through, modifying, or retrieving a real customer account.<br />\</Warning\><br />

A demo convenience mechanism may simplify authentication or reset state.

That mechanism terminates inside the demo tenant.

It never becomes a generic authorization bypass.

## Relational tenant integrity

Authorization protects operations.

RLS protects row access.

The database schema must also prevent impossible cross-tenant relationships.

Invalid states include:

```text
```

```text
Organization A
    ↓
Business A
    ↓
Site references Business B
from Organization B
```

and:

```text
```

```text
Lead
Organization A

references

Customer
Organization B
```

Tenant-owned relationships preserve Organization consistency through database constraints.

[Domain Model & Database Schema](/architecture/foundation/domain-model-and-database-schema) owns those constraints.

**Cross-tenant relationships should be invalid data, not merely discouraged application behavior.**

## Security boundary tests

Tenant isolation is a release requirement.

It is not accepted because the application appears to hide another customer's records.

V1 must prove:

- <br />Tenant A cannot read Tenant B data.<br />
- <br />Tenant A cannot insert data under Tenant B.<br />
- <br />Tenant A cannot update Tenant B data.<br />
- <br />Tenant A cannot delete Tenant B data.<br />
- <br />A Business ID owned by Tenant B is rejected under Tenant A authority.<br />
- <br />Changing a Business, Site, Lead, Customer, or other object ID cannot cross the tenant boundary.<br />
- <br />Tenant A cannot create relationships pointing to Tenant B objects.<br />
- <br />Background jobs remain scoped to their declared Organization.<br />
- <br />Provider Commands remain scoped to their owning Organization.<br />
- <br />Webhook mappings resolve to one known Organization before domain processing.<br />
- <br />Internal operations enter explicit tenant context.<br />
- <br />The public demo cannot access customer data.<br />
- <br />The public demo cannot operate customer provider resources.<br />

These tests verify the actual server and database boundary rather than trusting frontend behavior.

## Acceptance conditions

Accounts, Tenancy & Identity is working when:

- <br />A User can authenticate through Supabase Auth.<br />
- <br />Authentication alone does not grant access to an Organization.<br />
- <br />A User reaches an Organization through a current Membership.<br />
- <br />Organization is the tenant boundary for customer-owned state.<br />
- <br />Business remains distinct from Organization.<br />
- <br />A Business ID supplied by the browser cannot establish tenant authority.<br />
- <br />Tenant A cannot read or modify Tenant B data.<br />
- <br />Tenant A cannot create a relationship pointing to Tenant B data.<br />
- <br />Customer-scoped tenant tables are protected by appropriate RLS.<br />
- <br />Application operations still carry explicit Organization scope.<br />
- <br />Removing a Membership removes Organization authority even if the User remains authenticated.<br />
- <br />Privileged Supabase credentials never reach browser code.<br />
- <br />A privileged background operation resolves explicit Organization context before touching tenant data.<br />
- <br />A webhook resolves Organization through trusted provider relationships before domain processing.<br />
- <br />Internal Rule & Form access enters explicit tenant context before changing customer-owned state.<br />
- <br />Rule & Form staff do not require customer passwords or customer sessions.<br />
- <br />Provider IDs never replace Rule & Form Organization or Business identity.<br />
- <br />Website visitors and contractor customers do not become Rule & Form application Users.<br />
- <br />Business contact data remains separate from authentication data.<br />
- <br />Demo identity cannot enter a customer Organization.<br />
- <br />Tenant isolation is tested at the server and database boundary rather than inferred from the UI.<br />

## Foundation rules

1. **Authentication, authorization, and tenancy are separate concerns.**<br />
2. **Supabase Auth owns authentication mechanics. Rule & Form owns application authority and tenant meaning.**<br />
3. **Organization is the tenant boundary.**<br />
4. **Business belongs to an Organization. It is not a second tenant boundary.**<br />
5. **Users reach Organizations through current Membership or explicit Rule & Form internal authority.**<br />
6. **Knowing an Organization, Business, Site, Lead, Customer, or provider ID does not grant authority over it.**<br />
7. **Every tenant-owned operation resolves explicit Organization scope.**<br />
8. **Business-scoped operations verify Business ownership inside the authorized Organization.**<br />
9. **Customer-scoped access uses explicit tenant scoping and PostgreSQL RLS together.**<br />
10. **RLS strengthens database containment. It does not remove tenant awareness from application code.**<br />
11. **Membership remains Rule & Form authorization state rather than being reduced to user-editable or cached authentication metadata.**<br />
12. **Privileged Supabase credentials remain server-only.**<br />
13. **A privileged credential is never tenant authority.**<br />
14. **Background work carries explicit Organization context.**<br />
15. **Webhooks resolve tenant ownership through trusted provider relationships before domain processing.**<br />
16. **Internal Rule & Form authority and customer Membership remain separate domains.**<br />
17. **Internal privilege grants the ability to enter tenant context. It does not remove tenant context.**<br />
18. **Website visitors and contractor customers are not application Users.**<br />
19. **Provider identities and credentials never become Rule & Form identity.**<br />
20. **Authentication identity and Business Record facts remain separate.**<br />
21. **Database relationships preserve Organization consistency across tenant-owned objects.**<br />
22. **The public demo is confined to synthetic tenant state and dedicated provider resources.**<br />
23. **Cross-tenant isolation is tested directly.**<br />
24. **V1 stays narrow without collapsing distinctions the later product will need.**<br />

**Know the actor. Resolve the Organization. Verify the authority. Then touch the data.**
